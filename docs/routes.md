# Routes, reach and the bots that run them

Every bonus on the hand-built maps is driven end to end by a bot in `headless_run`, which means the geometry, the zones, the splits and the end line are proved by something other than reading them. The imported maps have a route bot and a replay follower of their own, at the end of this file.

## What a jump reaches

`G2GReach` holds this genre's two reaches, read off the `DotFpsTunables` the server applies (apex, climb limit, airtime and RUN reach are `DotFpsTunables.jump_apex`/`climb_limit`/`jump_airtime`/`jump_reach`, asked in metres and converted by `G2GUnits`), so `sv_gravity` and `sv_enablebunnyhopping` move every answer with no second number. Only the CHAIN is this game's own arithmetic.

- **RUN** is a jump from the lip at run speed. Ground acceleration gets a player back to 250 u/s in about thirty units, so anybody who lands anywhere on a block can run to its edge. It clears **189 u flat, 166 u onto a 24-u step, 222 u down 48**. The landing height is the whole point: the airtime everybody writes down is the time back to the height you left.
- **CHAIN** is a hop inside a run of auto-hops, a different quantity rather than a longer one. With the key held there is no grounded tick to walk to the lip on, so a hop takes off where the last one landed and the hops must average a whole block period (gap *and* block). A chain is judged by simulating a **perfect strafe** every tick (`v² + 30²`, `DotFpsMotor.accelerate`'s own arithmetic). That is an upper bound on purpose: a chain it refuses is one nobody can run. How much of a perfect strafe a chain needs is printed, not asserted, because no threshold on it would be anything but a guess.

**A map declares which bodies are a route and which reach it is for.** `G2GMap.add_course(name, track, kind, bodies)` is called from `_build` with the bodies `G2GGeometry.box` just returned; rise, clear air and landing depth are read off their transforms, so the declaration cannot drift from the geometry because it *is* the geometry. Clear air is the least distance between footprints (the stages map's zigzag is flown diagonally). No hull credit is spent: the capsule can stand about eleven units past a lip, and that is the margin. `surf_g2g_intro` declares nothing and says why in its header.

**`!s2` and `!s3` on `bhop_g2g_intro` put a player where nobody can continue.** A stage restart is a chain that begins at run speed rather than at what the stages before it built; from block 14 the next gap is 275 u and a perfect single hop from 250 u/s covers 245. The restarts are printed, not asserted. The only fixes are moving a stage line on a scored track or putting `!s3` somewhere that is not stage 3, which is Christian's call.

**With `sv_enablebunnyhopping 0`, neither bhop map's main route exists**: the landing cap trims every take-off to 275 u/s and one hop of strafing cannot span the period. That is asserted, which is also the proof that the chain arithmetic refuses something.

## How the bots drive

- **A scripted bot cannot strafe, so it cannot gain**, so every bonus a bot drives is a RUN route: each gap sized with `G2GReach` and at least ~29 units inside it, so it asks for a line and never for speed a player has not got.
- **Jumped AT the gaps, not held.** A bot holding jump with auto-hop leaves the ground on the tick it lands, never gets a grounded `accelerate` tick, and bleeds to `sv_maxairwishspeed` (30 u/s). Every bot presses jump only in the last 20 to 30 units of a block, read off the **map's** constants, so moving a block moves the jump with it.
- **A gap a standing jump clears is not a gap a chain of them clears.** A chained hop takes off wherever the last one landed, so the distance available is the jump minus how far onto the block the bot arrived. The needle's gap is 96 rather than 160 for that reason.
- `_test_bhop_run`'s 240 u/s while holding jump is the **prestrafe** carried into the first hop. True, and not evidence that the bot is running.
- **Pace is asserted** (at least 80% of run speed) on the newer routes, so a route that only works at a crawl fails.
- **Count resets in an `Array`, never a captured local.** A GDScript lambda captures a local by value, so `reset = true` inside a signal handler never reached the loop, and three route checks "passed without a reset" whatever happened. All use `var reset: Array[bool] = [false]`.
- A bot that aims at a point just past a lip hovers there and falls (a bot that has reached its target stops); aim at the next block's exit instead.

## Imported maps: a route written as data

The line a map is run along is not in the `.bsp`, so it is written per map in `maps/routes/<id>.json`: points in the manifest's units and axes, passed in order before the finish. `game/g2g_route_bot.gd` drives it (in `game/` since the server's bots fall back on it), shared by `tools/stage_ride.tscn -- <id> 0 <seconds>` and `headless_imported`'s route section.

- **The bot strafes, and in the air it aims its landing**: it works out the horizontal velocity that brings it down onto the next point's height over the next point, and spends its wish turning its velocity into that one. Under `sv_airaccelerate 1000` a wish can change the velocity by nearly anything in a tick, so it can brake in the air too.
- **A point counts only once the bot has landed on or past it** (`land`, default true): aiming at the next block from the air above this one commits to a jump it never took off for.
- On the ground it runs at the point and jumps at a lip or a step too tall to walk (`ledge_ahead`, `wall_ahead`), so a chained hop never starts mid-block. A `door` point is passed by the teleport.
- **`tools/route_plan.py <id> --reach 280 --out maps/routes/<id>.json` writes a route from the collision**: brush tops (and 64-unit terrain cells) are nodes, joined when the gap is within reach, the rise at most 54, the line clear at body height and a drop's landing column open; TELEPORT doors join tops to the top under their destination; Dijkstra from the spawn to the END. A point is the centre of a **brush** top, never a block's outline (L and U blocks have holes in the middle).
- **Zones are a graph (2026-11-03, `g2g-maps-1`)**: with the `.bsp` beside (`../inspirations/g2gfast/<id>.bsp`, or `G2G_BSP`) the planner reads the map's own `trigger_teleport`s, which the manifest does not keep (it keeps only the arrivals, as STAGE zones, and inflated RESPAWN boxes that overlap real floors). A gate (a trigger above a floor, destination the next section) is a door edge; a trigger a little over a floor (8 to 96 u) is a pit, and that floor is not a place to stand. Entity brush bounds are relative to the entity's `origin`. Long tops are cut into tiles (96 u, coarser on a map-sized floor) with the covered test per tile, because a centre-to-centre line across a long wall-top crosses walls no hop does. `--debug` prints the doors and, when no route is found, the frontier. Without the `.bsp` it falls back to the manifest TELEPORT zones.

## A recorded run, followed with the real movement

A ghost writes each frame's pose and passes through walls, proving nothing about the map. `tools/replay_follow.gd` drives the motor with ordinary `DotFpsCommand`s along a dot-timer replay (`maps/routes/<id>.replay`) and reports where a run can no longer be repeated: *"left the recorded line at frame N"*, which is where to look for a collision fault. `headless_imported`'s recording section follows the map's recording to a finish, then follows a copy lifted 200 u from frame 2000 and asserts the report names that frame. `tools/follow_replay.tscn -- <id>` is the same as a tool; `RIDE_SAVE_REPLAY=<path>` on `stage_ride` saves a route bot's run as one (it takes the replay on `player_finished`, because dot-timer drops `last_replay` the moment no store keeps the run).

The genre's air physics decides how the follower must steer, and a "pull back onto the line" controller cannot work:

- **A wish near the velocity adds nothing.** Air acceleration adds speed along the wish only up to 30 u/s past what the velocity already has along it; a turn is a wish at right angles to the velocity.
- **The same holds on the ground above run speed** (a landing is 270 to 360 u/s against a run of 250).
- **Short of speed, strafe at full wish toward the side the line bends**: it gains and turns at once, which is what a player's strafe is.
- **A jump is copied by place and held until airborne**, and within 24 frames of a recorded landing the air aims at the landing itself.

bhop_grove's recording is a bot's, recorded on the current motor. A person's record replay copied to `maps/routes/<id>.replay` is the line the follower was built for; none exists yet.

## A surf bot planned on the motor

The route bot's surf half (look along the face, strafe into it) has never ridden an imported surf stage, and `stage_ride`'s copy of it wedges on the apex of surf_beginner's first ramp for forty seconds: from the deck it heads straight down the middle, lands on the ridge between the two faces and strafes into one face and then the other. A surf line is not something a rule finds, so this bot does not have one: it searches for the line on the real motor, offline, and follows what it found. The reasoning, the alternatives and the numbers are in the family's study (`game-dev/docs/surf-bots-study.md`, outside the repositories).

The generic half lives in the addons since 2026-10-13, so game-arena's bots surf with the same code: the simulator is dot-player-controller's `DotFpsSimulator` (`controller.simulator()`, its `docs/motor.md`, "Simulating ahead") and the planner, the pilot and the course base are dot-bot's (`DotBotSurfPlanner`, `DotBotSurfPilot`, `DotBotSurfCourse`, its `docs/surfing.md`). What stays here is the adapter, `game/g2g_surf_course.gd`.

- **The simulator** is a second `DotFpsMotor` over the same physics space, with copied tunables, the same modifiers and the same volume modes, and its own query-only body excluding the player's collider. A simulated tick changes nothing live. `headless_imported`'s surf section checks that, that a ride repeats exactly, and that the live player given a cached ride's commands ends where the simulation did (0.0000 m after 486 ticks down surf_beginner's stage 1). The map's mechanics (`G2GMapMechanics`) are its `mechanics` hook, run on a rider that is not branched with the state; nothing on the stages planned so far needs that branched.
- **Actions are relative to the velocity**: a wish at an angle off the heading (nine of them, coast and jump included), view along the velocity, made with the strafe and forward keys. So one remembered action means the same thing at 600 and 900 u/s.
- **`G2GSurfCourse`** (extends `DotBotSurfCourse`) reads a stage from the timer's zones: the start is the stage zone's destination, the goal is the next stage's zone, the finish and every door into either, a pit is every RESPAWN/SLAY zone and every other door. Its score is progress along the start-to-goal line plus half the energy (height plus v²/2g). `simulator_for(controller, mechanics)` builds the simulator with this course as its oracle.
- **The planner** is a beam search (32 wide, actions held 8 ticks, one survivor per 48-unit cell and speed bucket) that ends at the first child in a goal. Then **repair rounds**: the pilot rides from many perturbed starts, and every ride that fails is searched again from a second before it failed and added, so the cache grows where the pilot goes wrong.
- **The pilot** takes the action of the cached state nearest its own (position plus a quarter-second of velocity, on a 128-unit grid) and runs that policy 192 ticks ahead on the simulator: a future in a pit is vetoed for the action whose future survives. Since the move the forecast is kept and extended a tick at a time while the real ride stays on it (`rolling`), and the lookup grid is really built (the prototype's grid was always empty, see game-dev's `gdscript-hazards.md`): 97 of 100 simulated and 39 of 40 live perturbed starts arrive, at 1.6 motor ticks of lookahead a ridden tick (136 us) where the prototype ran 7.3 (660 us) for 94 and 38. `--no-rolling` measures the old veto. A `tracking` mode is better on a sparse cache and worse on a repaired one; it is off.
- **`maps/routes/<id>.surf/<stage>.json`** is the cache, one file per stage (a stage's file stays well under the 1 MB cap however many stages a map has; the old single `<id>.surf.json` is still read, a stage file winning, and `tools/split_surf_cache.gd -- <id>` splits one; `headless_bots` proves both layouts read the same data): every tick of every ride as `[x, y, z, vx, vy, vz, action]` in genre units. `tools/surf_plan.tscn -- <id> <stage>` writes it and prints the measurements; regenerate after a movement or collision change, which `headless_imported` will show as the surf bot no longer arriving.
- **Cached stages**: surf_beginner 1 and 2 (stage 2 about 85% simulated, 90% live). A stage whose line bends far from the start-to-goal line carries a `guide` polyline (genre units) in its cache entry, and `G2GSurfCourse.progress` measures along it on the floor plan only: the base class's 3D projection let a line falling off a ramp score as if it had finished. Stage 2 uses `--guide "2400,250,-3400;1068,56,-3968"`. ## Bots on a server

`game/g2g_bots.gd` (`G2GGame.bots`, built on every authoritative game) holds the server's bots; how many is dot-server's `DotBotQuota` (`bot_quota`, `bot_quota_mode`, `bot_quota_empty`), kept in step by `G2GModule._keep_bots` twice a second. **`bot_quota` defaults to 0 here**, unlike the family's other games: a records server is run for people, and a bot riding a stage is a demonstration an operator chooses.

- **What a bot rides is the map's.** With a surf cache, the cache's first stage with dot-bot's `DotBotSurfPilot` over the shared lookup, on a simulator copied from its own motor. Without one, the whole run (stage 0, spawn to finish) with the rule-based route bot (`g2g_route_bot.gd`, along `maps/routes/<id>.json` when the map has one): on a surf ramp that is "look along the face, strafe into it", which wedges on surf_beginner's first ridge, and it is what there was before the pilot. A map with no course at all leaves them standing at the spawn.
- **A bot has no timer** (`G2GGame.add_player(..., bot = true)`, `G2GPlayer.is_bot`), no statistics and no achievements, so nothing it does is filed. The zones therefore never move it; `G2GSurfCourse.event_at` does: in the goal, in a pit, under the course, or 30 s without arriving, and it is put back at the stage's start facing the way `!s<n>` faces a player, with a fresh pilot ride. Commands go in before the moves (`G2GGame._simulate_tick`).
- **No forecast on a live server** (`G2GBots.veto_horizon` 0). A bot always starts its ride at the same exact state, so the ride is the same every time and the veto never fires on it; the forecast still cost 36 ms in one tick at every ride start and a veto twelve times that, against a 7.8 ms tick. Measured at 128 Hz on surf_beginner stage 1 (`headless_bots`, and an offline game): **93 us a tick per bot** with the cache alone, 339 us (worst tick 36 ms) with the 192-tick veto; every ride arrives in 4.05 s either way. Before dot-bot's lookup skipped far cells (2026-10-13) the same were 374 us and 912 us (worst 79 ms). What is lost is the recovery after somebody knocks a bot off its line: it falls and is put back.
- **A map with no route file is planned, not aimed** (`game/g2g_bot_nav.gd`, `bot_nav` cvar, default 1). dot-npc-ai's `DotNpcAiNavAnnotator` finds the links (drop, jump up, jump gap) on a baked mesh; the planner joins them to the mesh's polygons in an `AStar3D` (a link is a node of its own with a weight over 1: drop 1.5, jump up 3, gap 4), drops every polygon and link in a pit or under the floor (the course would put the bot back from it, and on a bhop map most of the mesh is the pit floor), and turns the way from the spawn to the polygon nearest the goal into route-bot points: thinned walk points, and a ledge and a landing for each link (a gap's ledge is `hop`). A gap is judged at 2.5 times running speed, because the route bot chains hops. **Never in a tick**: the colliders are parsed in one engine call, the bake runs on a worker thread, the annotator, the graph and the search are cursors stepped for 2 ms of each tick (`NAV_BUDGET_USEC`), and the annotations are cached by their own key in `user://g2g_nav`. Until the plan is adopted the bots go straight at the finish as before, so planning is never a wait. Without dot-npc-ai in the project (a delivered shell may not carry it) the planner is off and nothing changes.
- **What it measured** (`headless_bots`, farthest a bot got from its start in 20 s, as the crow flies, `bot_nav` 0 then 1): bhop_fur 15.1 m to 55.9 m, bhop_platformer_fxd 10.0 m to 14.9 m, bhop_elate 10.9 m to 11.7 m. Maps whose gaps are laid out for a player at bhop speed (bhop_aztec, bhop_sqee, bhop_badges_mini) are no better: the annotator finds no link out of the first island, and the route ends there. A staged map's finish is behind doors the graph does not know, so the route ends at the reachable polygon nearest it.
- **Cost.** Cold, on a 7 to 19 thousand polygon map: the bake 1 to 2 s on a worker, the annotation 2 to 18 s and the graph 1 to 3 s of main-thread work spread over ticks; from cache the annotation is free. One step is not small: a single item of the annotator overran at 30 to 100 ms on these maps (the slice is already 1), which a server feels as one long tick; that is dot-npc-ai's to split.
- **The driving cost is in `describe()`** (`g2g_status`): per bot, rides, arrivals, falls, stuck rides, the best time, and microseconds a driven tick, mean and worst, counted from zero on each map.

**Chained first leg (2026-10-27, `g2g-beginner-stage2-1`, five planner runs, no route cached)**: `surf_plan --chain "x,y,z,hx,hy,hz,min_u/s" --guide ... --guide2 ... [--height N --height2 N]` plans the stage start to a goal box, then the stage's own search from the state the first leg arrives in, and saves both as one ride (nothing is saved unless both legs are found). `G2GSurfCourse.event_at` now takes a `goal_fn` and treats a fall faster than 2400 u/s as a pit (the stuck-fall exploit; stateless, not the 20-tick rule, because no state history exists in the search).
