# The client: camera, input, HUD, menus and presentation

`G2GClient` is one local player and is never loaded by a server. Every choice here is downstream of one sentence: **a player who came to run must not be stopped by anything added for a player who came to do something else.** No assertion sees the screen, so every change here wants a frame rendered (`tools/screenshot_hud.sh`, `tools/screenshot_menus.sh`, under `xvfb-run`).

## The genre's field of view, not Godot's

`fov_desired 90` is 90° **horizontal at 4:3**, widened on a wider screen. Godot's `Camera3D.fov` is vertical and fixed. `G2GUnits.horizontal_fov_to_vertical` converts through the 4:3 frame: 90 becomes 73.74° vertical, which on 16:9 shows 106.26° horizontally, exactly what the genre shows. Handing 90 straight to Godot gives 121° at 16:9 and a player who cannot aim and cannot say why. The setting is `field_of_view`, the genre's 90 over 75..120.

`sensitivity` has its usual meaning: degrees per count is `sensitivity × 0.022`, so a player's 2.5 works unchanged.

## The camera, the rig and the player's own body

`G2GCamera` keeps both cameras and flips `current` (third person is cosmetic: see [movement.md](movement.md#third-person-is-cosmetic-and-stays-g2gcameras)). The controller aims from the eye whichever is active.

- **The rig is on two visibility layers** so the owner's first-person camera can cull it while every other camera draws it. Layers rather than `visible`, because a culled first-person player still needs their shadow, the only cue a first-person view has for how tall they are and where they stand. `G2GRig._set_layers_recursive` must set only the layer `visible_to_owner` asks for: setting both once drew the player's own avatar at point-blank range over the bottom third of every frame, with every number correct. `headless_presentation` checks it with one bitwise AND against the camera's cull mask.
- **`show_own_body` is a third state, not "first person with the cull off".** The body mount is 40 cm below the eye; the head and hat mounts sit *at* the eye. Showing the whole rig puts the camera inside a 40 cm cube. `G2GRig.owner_sees_head` is a second flag, and *nothing* / *body only* / *everything* are settled in one place, `G2GPlayer._apply_own_body`.
- **The default is applied where the camera is built**, not corrected once a frame from `present()`: a `_process` body aborts on a script error and the game carries on, so any frame that did not finish drew the player's own avatar.
- **A spectator's camera is driven after the camera-shake line in `_process`**, which sets the active camera's local position every frame; in the other order a spectator looked through the watched player's view from their own eyes. In first person the watched body is not drawn (`_watched_from_inside`).

## Avatars: one schema, two sources of parts

`G2GAvatars.schema()` is a real `DotAvatarSchema` (body, head, hat) with one attachment node per slot on the rig. **A stock character and a player's own platform avatar are the same thing at different addresses**: stock parts ship under `res://avatars/`, a platform part names dot-cloud content, and `DotAvatarBuilder` does not care. A player with no avatar gets `stock_avatar(id)`, deterministic in the id so the same player is the same colour on every machine.

- **`DotAvatarSchema.conform()` edits the document in place.** `G2GAvatars.apply` runs it on a copy, or a server that could not draw one part would rewrite a player's own avatar. A part from a newer client is dropped, never refused.
- **Players are the family's animated people** (dot-player-char's `DotHumanoidFigure`, its docs/people.md), coloured from the avatar document by `G2GAvatars.look_of` (a Kenney body part gives that skin's clothes, a Kenney head that skin's face, hair and body; a primitive part its tint), at the genre's 72-unit hull (1.372 m). `G2GRig.dress` still builds the document onto the mounts (so a newer client's part is conformed away, and the result counts the slots), then lets the body and head parts go and keeps the hat, `HAT_DROP` under the person's full height. The person is told the drawn state every frame (`G2GRig.move`: speed, vertical, floor, crouch), so a crouch is the person's crouch clip, not the old squash of the rig; the owner's own body in first person hides its head bone (`hide_head`), the person being one mesh. `avatars/{body,head}_kenney_<a..r>.tscn` (generated by `tools/build_avatar_parts.gd`) remain the document's parts; the two primitives stay at the FRONT of `BODIES` and `HEADS`: `[0]` is the schema default, so a deployment without the art still resolves a part.
- The body is foot-aligned and the head centred; the rig mounts at 42% of the hull and **is not changed for the art**, because game-arena mounts the same way. `tools/avatar_preview.tscn` draws parts inside a wireframe of the hull they must fit: "is this the right size" is not a question an eyeball on a character alone can answer.

## Input: one table of keys, and no `[input]` block

**There is no `[input]` block in `project.godot`, deliberately.** `DotFpsSampler` keys off its own `dot_fps_*` actions and `register_default_actions` adds them at runtime. A game that depended on its own actions would have no controls in dot-server-deploy's client shell, whose `project.godot` has none.

**`G2GBindings.ROWS` is the one list of keys** (a `DotMenuBindings` through `G2GBindings.make()`), applied at runtime from the settings document and rebindable in the Escape menu. Four things read it: the client's event matching, the settings schema, the Controls page and the help screen (which shows the key each action is on *now*). The movement rows ARE the sampler's `dot_fps_*` actions, so there is no second copy.

- Duck is **Ctrl**, not Shift. Tab is the scoreboard (held); Next style is N.
- Bindings are DEVICE scope (a key is a fact about a keyboard), except the two chat keys, which were ACCOUNT before the table existed and keep it: changing a stored setting's scope resets somebody's key.
- A binding that names nothing falls back to the default; binding a taken key SWAPS the two. Escape is in no row and cannot be bound: it is the menu, and how a browser releases the pointer.
- **A settings migration that drops a default drops exactly that value.** A settings document stores defaults too, so every player who never chose has the old default written down: schema 2 drops a stored `field_of_view` of exactly 110 (the old meaningless default), schema 3 a `bind_style_next` of exactly "Tab". Widening it would overwrite real choices.

## The pointer: asked for, never told

Pointer lock in a browser needs **transient user activation** (a real click), and `_ready()` is the one moment guaranteed not to have one. It is refused *silently*: `Input.mouse_mode` reads back CAPTURED and the cursor wanders out of the window mid-run.

- Desktop captures immediately. Web shows "Click to play" and captures on the first mouse press, handled **before** the `player == null` guard, because a browser player usually clicks while the world is still loading.
- **Escape releases and never toggles**, on both platforms: a browser itself exits pointer lock on Escape and refuses to re-enter for about a second, so a toggle silently does nothing every other press.
- The browser half of this (opening the menu when the lock is taken away, swallowing the Escape that follows, recapturing on Resume, `navigator.keyboard.lock()` in fullscreen so Ctrl+W while ducking does not close the tab) is dot-menu's now.
- Playwright cannot move a pointer-locked mouse (`movementX`/`Y` are 0 on synthetic events). Test the thing beside it that shares the failure (F5 under the same guard), and check pointer lock with a real click in headless Chromium.

## Menus are dot-menu's; the pages are this game's

The Escape menu, help, the map list, "Move HUD elements", the key rebinder, the themes, the frame-rate counter, the browser pointer handling and the stock settings with their applier are **dot-menu**'s, extracted from this game. What stays here: `game/ui/g2g_menu_pages.gd` (which rows go where, in the genre's words, and the live rows for style, flashlight and third person), `G2GBindings`' rows, `G2GPresentation`'s own settings and defaults, and the `menu_*` methods on the client the live rows ask. **The client shell must be built with dot-menu**, or no script here that names a `DotMenu*` class parses. The page check accepts dot-menu's optional trailing `advanced` page.

- **The menu is a view of the settings, never a copy**: `G2GPresentation._on_setting_changed` and `G2GClient.apply_client_settings` are the only places a setting is applied, at boot and on change alike; there is no Apply button.
- **Style, flashlight and third person are not settings**: the server has a say in each, and a stored "on" would be a promise the next server can refuse.
- **It does not pause**; the sampler is suspended, so a runner who opens it stands still.
- **Every declared setting must be applied by something.** Five once were not (sensitivity, field of view, show speed, show splits, preferred style); `raw_input` was removed for the same reason.
- **The Tab board is a timer server's**: style, best time and rank on this map, time connected and ping, fastest first. Online, names, pings and times are dot-server's roster (sent only while a player holds Tab) and the columns are `G2GModule`'s `scoreboard_fields` from `G2GGame.board_fields`, the standing already worked out for the HUD, so a held board costs no store reads. The module sets the hooks only on a dot-server that has them and takes them back on unload.
- `tools/screenshot_menus.sh` keeps its settings in memory: a client writes `user://g2g_settings` on every change, and a tool that flips the theme would otherwise change the player's own game.

## Solo in the browser shell: Leave and the course list

Inside the shell's solo session (a `dot_solo` service in `DotRegistry`, duck-typed; see `G2GSoloMaps`) Leave reads "Back to menu" and calls the session's `leave()`, because a page cannot close its tab; anywhere else offline it is still "Quit game" on desktop and hidden on web. The same service makes M list every g2gfast course in dot-server-deploy's `cfg.example/content.yml` (kept in `game/g2g_solo_maps.gd`, and `headless_client` fails when the two drift), fetched under owner `gamemann` the first time one is chosen, the way the first map is. The browser itself was not driven for this change.

## The load gate: nothing is shown or timed until the level is in

`G2GClient` holds a dot-core `DotLoadGate` (built first in `_ready`): held for the map (again on every map change), `spawned` until the local player and its camera exist, and the local body frozen with `gate.freeze`. Before it, solo drew the body falling onto a floor still arriving and a connected client drew a HUD with nobody to read, and the timers were fed meanwhile. Three things honour it: `G2GGame.covered` (set by the client) stops the whole tick except the local body, which settles under the cover with a neutral command, so no run, start zone, bot or hunter acts while covered; `G2GPlayer.simulate` skips a frozen body and reads no keys while settling; the client's keys and the netcode sampler check `_covered()`. `G2GPlayer` answers `is_on_floor()` and `velocity` because the gate asks a settling body for them by name, and without them it lifts the cover with the body still in the air. `tools/gate_shot.gd` (xvfb) prints gate state, height and timer ticks per frame and saves frames.

## The HUD

`G2GHud` draws the clock, the keys, speed in u/s, the standing line, PRACTICE, the spectator list and an admin's blind.

- **The clock's block is `DotTimerHud`'s own fields**, so a line switched off closes the block up. Positions are saved as `hud_positions`: each piece's **centre as a fraction of the window**, so a layout made on a laptop lands in the same place on a large screen; unknown or off-screen entries are dropped or clamped back. An empty clock still has a size to grab. Presets write settings rather than being one.
- **The stage splits panel is dot-timer's `DotTimerSplitsPanel`** (`G2GHud.splits_panel`), on a map with stage lines only: one row per section (n lines, n + 1 rows, the last called Finish), at the left edge and vertically centred, clear of the clock's block (bottom centre by default), the notice line and the status line. The setting is `show_stage_splits` (Heads-up display, on by default; distinct from `show_splits`, the clock block's one split line), and what it compares with is the existing `timer_comparison` ("Split against": your best, the record, nothing), so there is one choice for both. It is fed twice: `set_reference` from `refresh_stage_reference()`'s moments (map change, a filed run or section via `G2GGame.stage_reference_changed`, a track or style change, a setting) and `show_run` every frame. **Offline, and on a listen server, the reference is `G2GGame.stage_reference(id)`** (store peeks, never waits); **a connected client ignores its own store** (a file store of solo bests, not the server's), so the server sends it a STAGE_REF ([netcode](netcode.md#wire-rules)) and `G2GHud.apply_stage_reference` keeps it, used only while its track and style are still the player's. **While spectating the panel is hidden**: the reference is the owner's best and section bests, and a watched player's run against them compares nobody with nobody.
- **The time left is the vote's clock**, adopted from CLOCK events into one `clock_view` and counted down between messages; with no clock the slot is gone (see [services.md](services.md#the-vote)). Offline, where the local session is the real clock, it keeps the session's.
- **While spectating**, the clock, speed and keys are the watched player's (`G2GHud.followed_id`); the standing line stays the owner's, because a client is only told its own.
- **The blind overlay** fades in under the clock and keys, sized to the viewport rather than to the HUD, and is the HUD's first child.
- **Hit feedback on combat maps is dot-ui's** `DotHitMarker` and `DotDamageNumbers`, flashed by `confirm_hit` from a hit the authority applied and never from the local prediction (a marker for a shot the server refused teaches a player to ignore it). `G2GCombat.hit_landed` says it: offline the HUD hears it directly, online the server sends a HIT event to the shooter alone. A refused hit, one that took no health or one on yourself is no hit. The run timer is not a `DotScoreStrip` clock and stays the timer's.
- **The HUD is in the menu's theme twice over**: `G2GHud.apply_theme` (the timer and the lines, outlined in the theme's ink) runs on the menu's `theme_changed`, and dot-menu's `follow_hud(hud, null, false)` themes dot-ui's pieces on it (the hit marker, the damage numbers, the ammunition counter). **It is not scaled by `hud_scale`** (the `false`): the zone editor lays this HUD out in frame pixels, and a scaled HUD would put its frames somewhere else.
- `tools/screenshot_hud.sh` renders the clock, keys, stage line, notices (`--splits` renders the stage splits panel mid-run on bhop_g2g_stages), the clock states, beacon, a confirmed headshot with the weapon in hand, blind and the gate effects (`--fx`).

## The flashlight lights unshaded surfaces too

Every imported map is `unshaded`, so a `SpotLight3D` lit nothing a player plays on. Both BSP shaders compute the cone from a 4x1 float texture `G2GFlashlight` rewrites each frame and sets as the **shader's default** for `flashlight_tex`: one write for every surface, and **not a `global uniform`, which must be declared in the host project's settings or the shader does not compile**; this game is delivered into a shell whose `project.godot` it does not own. `hint_default_black` means a server, a suite and an older pack draw as before. A spotlight still lights the shaded half. Client-only; the server's say is `sv_flashlight`, in RULES.

**RULES** carries `sv_flashlight`, `sv_allow_thirdperson` and the chat commands as JSON, on admit and on change. The command list is read off the server's console (`G2GModule.chat_commands`: public, chat-reachable, sorted), so the help screen is never a list somebody typed.

**Hiding the others (O) is drawing only**: every other `G2GPlayer`, the ghost included, gets `visible = false` and their beacon pings are not played; they are still simulated and replicated. The player being spectated is never hidden.

## Presentation: settings, sound and effects

`G2GPresentation` holds dot-settings, dot-audio, dot-fx and dot-console.

- **Camera shake defaults to zero and full-screen flashes to off** (every other game in the family defaults shake to one). A surf ramp is a precision input; shaking it for a landing takes the run away. Both stay settings, for the deathmatch layer.
- **The effect budget is 24**, small on purpose: nothing drawn may cost a 7.8 ms tick. Safe because an effect never changes the simulation.
- **The landing sound is the speedometer**: pitched by the landing speed, because a bunny-hop is a rhythm. It is **watched rather than listened for** (a landing fires on the authority, which on a netted client is elsewhere, so a hooked signal is silent online). `land` is an impact, not a thud with a tail, because a tail smears the rhythm.
- **Footsteps and a bed per map are dot-audio's stock**: `G2GPresentation.keep_walkers` hands it every rider, and `bed_for` picks the bed by map prefix. The landing stays this game's own sound (below), so the stock landing step is off. Levels are measured with `addons/dot_audio/tools/loudness.sh` and held in the family's −17 LUFS band through `loudness_trim_db`.
- **Effects default to 0.6 and the bed follows speed (fgr-g2g-audio-1)**: `sfx_volume` is declared here at 0.6 ahead of the stock 1.0. The bed's level runs from `snd_wind_min` (0.3, standing) to `snd_wind_max` (1.1) at `snd_wind_full_speed` (1500 units a second), linearly (`G2GPresentation.wind_level`), set every frame from `watch_movement`; all three are settings, so cvars.
- **No audio files ship.** `sound_recipes()` maps every catalogued sound to a `DotAudioSynth` voice and `DotAudioSinkGodot` falls through to that bank when a path resolves to nothing. A headless run has no audio device, so only `tools/audio_probe.sh` can tell "makes a noise" from "has a table of sounds".
- **The timer's sounds follow the client's own timer**: `follow_runs` listens to the client's `DotTimerManager` (fed on a netted client too), so the start sounds on the tick local prediction crosses the line. The personal-best fanfare is `record_accepted`, which only fires where records are filed; a netted client plays the finish without it rather than guessing. The vote's cues and the beacon ping play on voices the run does not use and below the timer's priority.
- **Gate effects** (`scenes/fx/start_gate.tscn`, `finish_gate.tscn`) are script-free scenes with no external resource, so a delivered pack has nothing in them to rewrite; `FX_DIR` goes through `G2GPaths.rebase`. **Every emitter is `local_coords`**: dot-fx adds a scene and then sets its transform, so a world-space emitter's first burst leaves from the world origin. `_build_fx` warns when a catalogued scene is missing (dot-fx refuses one only at DEBUG). In first person a runner mostly does not see their own start gate; it rises to knee height.
- **The console is dot-console's `DotConsoleKit`, built by `G2GPresentation.build_console(menu)` after the client's `DotMenu`**, so the panel is one of the menu's overlays and opening it suspends the sampler through `overlays_changed` (`headless_client` holds W to prove it).
- **The server's console is reached only as `server <line>` here** (unprefixed in every other game): `build_console` swaps the kit's bridge for `ServerPrefixed`, because `!s3` on a client is a local navigation and on a server a teleport that ends a run.

## The chat box, and a run nobody may lose to a keystroke

dot-ui's `DotChatWindow`, built by `G2GPresentation` in the bottom-left corner. `chat_window` (`auto` / `on` / `off`), `chat_open_key` (Y) and `chat_team_key` (U) are ACCOUNT-scoped. `auto` hides the box on a server already carrying chat somewhere the player can see it; only the server can answer that, so `G2GServices` points `DotChatManager.watch_relay` at its relay and `G2GClientExtras` turns the payload into `chat_relay_changed`. Off means "you type somewhere else", never "you are out of the conversation".

**Typing suspends the sampler as well as swallowing input.** `G2GPresentation.swallows_input()` keeps typed keys out of `_unhandled_input`; it can do nothing about `Input.is_action_pressed`, polled every physics frame. `_refresh_suspended` covers the offline player's own sampler as well as the networked one, and runs in `_adopt` so a player made while the box is open starts still. **Nobody may lose a personal best by saying "gg".**

## Zones drawn, and the zone editor

`G2GZoneOutlines` draws every zone as a glowing box (one MultiMesh of edge boxes plus an additive halo, so it does not depend on the environment's bloom; setting `show_zones`). **Z is the zone editor** (`G2GZoneEditor`): aim-placed corners snapped to 16 units, live preview, height on PgUp/PgDn. Offline it saves to `user://zones/<map>.json`, which `_on_map_changed` reads in preference to the map's own zones **on an authoritative game only** (a client never reads its own file over the server's). Online it sends `/g2g_zone`, `/g2g_zone_mark x y z height` and `/g2g_zone_save`; nothing has driven that path end to end, and zones an admin draws reach other clients only when they load the map with the file.
